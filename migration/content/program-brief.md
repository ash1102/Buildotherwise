## 1. Understand Build Otherwise
Build Otherwise is an emerging social-impact initiative founded by Ashley Christine Zeck.
Its work explores how people can discover possibilities beyond the circumstances, experiences, and identities that may have shaped their lives.
The initiative recognizes that difficult experiences and unconventional survival skills can sometimes reveal useful abilities, knowledge, or perspectives.
However, hardship is never treated as inherently beneficial, necessary, or something a person must transform into productivity.
People are not required to find meaning in suffering, demonstrate resilience, or build a business from their experiences.
Build Otherwise values:
- Personal agency and self-determination.
- Experiential learning and experimentation.
- Intellectual independence and critical thinking.
- Evidence over assumptions.
- Community without coercion.
- Multiple definitions of success.
- Recognition of real-world constraints.
- Opportunities to discover interests and capabilities without predetermined outcomes.
Its Scars to Strategy framework explores difficult experiences and unconventional strengths as possible assets, without requiring anyone to regard their hardship as worthwhile.
Build Otherwise is an emerging initiative. Its educational program is experimental, and effectiveness must not be presented as established or scientifically proven.

## 2. Understand Entrepalooza
Entrepalooza is Build Otherwise's flagship experimental educational program.
It combines: Self-discovery. Experiential learning. Reflection. Real-world experimentation. Exploration of interests and possibilities. Critical thinking and evidence gathering. Community and social connection. Optional entrepreneurship.
Entrepreneurship is one possible direction, never the required or preferred outcome.
A participant might discover an interest in employment, education, volunteering, creative work, community involvement, independent projects, entrepreneurship, or something entirely different.
They may also decide to pause, reconsider, or remain uncertain.
These are all legitimate outcomes.
The program is intended to provide opportunities for participants to discover their own direction, not tell them what their direction should be.

## 3. Understand the Educational Approach
Entrepalooza is informed by several complementary ideas:
Self-determination: Participants should retain meaningful control over their choices, exploration, and decisions.
Experiential learning: People can learn by trying things, observing what happens, reflecting, and deciding what to explore next.
Intrinsic motivation: Curiosity, interest, and enjoyment are meaningful signals worth investigating, without treating them as proof of a permanent passion or calling.
Evidence-based reflection: Participants should be able to distinguish what happened from what they believe it means.
Contextual understanding: Abilities and behavior are influenced by environments, resources, opportunities, and circumstances. A difficult experience with a task does not necessarily indicate a personal deficiency.
Respect for uncertainty: Not knowing something is information, not failure.
These are educational influences, not claims that Entrepalooza is a validated psychological intervention.
